Put an iPhone behind the approach with the whole lane in view. LaneIQ follows each ball and tells you what happened: speed, path, breakpoint, where it hit the pocket, spin, and how you moved. Nothing to put in the ball. Nothing to buy. No app store.

What it reads from the camera

Every number carries how far off it could be, and is marked when it was entered or estimated instead of measured.

The lane

Finds the lane by itself, from the foul line to the pins, in dim or uneven light, and from off to one side. If the lock is off, drag four dots to fix it.

The shot

Speed, boards at the foul line, arrows and breakpoint, launch and entry angle, and where the ball hit the pocket.

Spin

Rev rate, axis rotation and tilt, read from the shot's own video clip.

Your approach

Steps, timing, drift, laydown and finish from a pose model that runs on the phone.

The pins

Reads which pins fell and puts them straight on your scorecard. Tap the right number if it is wrong, and it learns.

The lane over time

After a few shots with the same ball it says whether the lane looks stable, drying out, or carrying down, and suggests one move to try.

Set up in a minute

  1. Open LaneIQ in Safari and tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It opens like an app and works at a center with no signal.
  2. Put the phone where the whole lane is in view, with both gutters, the foul line and the pins. A tripod or a stand on a table behind the approach works. The in-app guide has pictures.
  3. Tap Start camera. It locks onto the lane and follows each ball. Tap Stop between your shots in league so it only keeps yours.

Private by design

On the phone

Shots, videos and scores stay on the phone unless you send something yourself. Videos are never uploaded.

No ads, no tracking

There are no ads in the app. An account is optional and only backs up your shots and scores.

Works offline

Once it has opened, the app runs with no signal. Updates arrive quietly the next time you are online.

An honest note on accuracy

LaneIQ's lane finding and ball following have been tested on a large set of simulated lanes and deliveries, including dim, washed-out and uneven light, and in the tests it follows the ball down the lane reliably. It is still in open beta and it is being checked on real lanes: a phone's picture of a real center can differ from any test. If something is off at your center, the in-app Save frame and diagnostics tools let us fix it from a real example. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you send it.
