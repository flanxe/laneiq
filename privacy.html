LaneIQ privacy policy

Effective October 22, 2026. This is a plain-language policy for an app that keeps your data on your own phone and, if you leave backup on, in a private backup in your account.

The short version

LaneIQ keeps your data on your phone, and has no advertising and no analytics. You sign in with your email so you can pick up on a new phone. Your account holds your email address and, when backup is on (the default, and you can turn it off any time under Me), a backup of your profile, shots, balls, games and settings in a private folder only your account can open. Your videos and photos are never uploaded. We do not sell your data. You can delete your account, and every backup in it, from the Me tab.

Your account

To use LaneIQ you create an account with your email address. We email you a one-time code; there is no password. Your account is stored with our database provider, Supabase, which can see your email address and the backup files you save. A backup is made once a day while you use the app, or when you tap Back up now, and can be turned off under Me. Your backup contains your profile and what the app has measured and saved about your bowling, but not your videos or photos. Restoring on a new phone downloads it back and adds what is missing. Deleting your account permanently removes your sign-in and all of your backups. Bowlers under 13 need a parent or guardian to create the account with their own email address.

What is stored, and where

On your phone only (in the app's own storage): your profile (name, the hand you throw with, optional rev rate and the answers to the setup questions), your shots and their measurements, the video clips of your shots, the balls you add and photos' colour signatures used to recognise them, your scorecards, your settings, the target you set, the centre and lane you type, your drill results, the body and approach numbers read from your clips, the oil patterns you add from your centre's sheets, and a small log of how the lane lock and ball tracking behaved so you can copy it if you need help, and, when the lane lock fails or you correct it, a small picture of the camera view with the lane outline (shown in Me under Lane lock reports, with a button to delete them; they are only sent if you tap Send and choose where). It stays there until you delete it. Deleting a profile in the Me tab removes that profile's shots, videos and plan; clearing the website data for the app, or deleting the app, removes everything.

The camera and photos

The camera is used live to find the lane and follow the ball. The pictures are analysed on your phone and are not uploaded. Video clips of your shots are saved on your phone only. Photos you take for "Scan a ball" and the flare-ring photos are analysed on your phone and are not uploaded. The app asks for camera permission, and you can turn it off in your phone's settings at any time.

What goes over the internet

  • The app's own files. When you open LaneIQ it loads its code and its ball and oil-pattern lists from its web host (GitHub Pages). Like any website, the host can see your IP address and the time of the request. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • The text reader. The first time you use "Scan a ball" or add an oil pattern from a photo, a text-reading engine (tesseract.js) and its language data are downloaded from public content networks (jsDelivr and the Tesseract project). Those servers can see your IP address. Your photo is not sent. Pattern sheets you add as a PDF are read on your phone with no download at all.
  • The pose model. To draw the skeleton and read your approach, the app reads each new shot's clip this way (you can switch that off in the Coach tab, or use "Read my approach" shot by shot), and downloads Google's MediaPipe Pose Landmarker the first time: its code from jsDelivr and its model file from Google's model storage (storage.googleapis.com). Those servers can see your IP address. The model runs on your phone; your clips and the body positions it finds are not sent anywhere.
  • Friend requests and profile cards. A friend request is a link or a QR code you make on your phone. The link can carry a profile card with the groups of numbers you choose to share (ball speed and rev rate, entry angle, breakpoint and pocket rate, average and strike rate, the names of your balls) along with your name, hand and style. You see exactly what is in it before you send it, and nothing is sent until you send it yourself. A friend you send it to keeps that card on their phone until you send a newer one, and you keep theirs. There is no chat: messages to friends go through your phone's own messaging app. Scanning a QR code uses the camera and is done on your phone. Profile cards are switched off on a junior profile.
  • Crews. A crew is shared only by links you send. An update link carries the crew's name, its members' first names or account names and friend codes, their challenge results (numbers worked out from their own finished games), cheers from a short fixed set, and bowl nights with the place you type. It never carries video, location or full names. There is no public search, so nobody can find or join a crew without a link from a member. You can leave a crew at any time, which removes it from your phone. Crews are switched off on a junior profile.
  • Leagues and junior progress cards. League standings, a bowler's night, and a junior's progress card are links or pictures made on your phone and sent only if you send them through your phone's share sheet. They carry names as typed (first names or nicknames only for a junior), scores and averages, and never video or location. Leagues and the cards you receive are kept on your phone. A junior profile switches off friends, live sharing and data sharing. For a child under 13, a parent or guardian should set up the profile and decide who receives a card.
  • Sharing with a coach. The side-by-side packet, the Bowler DNA card and the highlight reel are made on your phone and only leave it if you send them yourself through your phone's share sheet.
  • Sharing with friends. Friend invites and shared sessions are links or messages you send yourself through your phone's share sheet. We do not run a server that receives them. Whatever you choose to put in a link (such as your name and friend code) goes to the people you send it to, through the app you send it with.

Finding a pro shop

When you tap "Find a pro shop near me", the app sends your position (or the city or ZIP code you typed) and a search distance to OpenStreetMap's services (Overpass and Nominatim), and, once LaneIQ's shop list is running, to that list. Those services can see your IP address. Nothing else about you is sent and the search is not saved. You choose whether to share your location; the app asks your phone for permission first.

Ordering from a pro shop

When you order through Pro shop mode, the order (your name, how to reach you, the ball, a suggested layout and the readings you chose to include) goes only to the shop, through the link or message you send. If you tick the box, your hand measurements and layouts on your other balls go too. Release video is never attached unless you send it yourself. The shop keeps a customer profile on its own phone.

Other people on camera

Bowling centres are shared spaces. Point the camera at your own lane and be considerate of other bowlers; follow the centre's rules about filming. Clips stay on your phone unless you choose to share one.

Children

LaneIQ is not aimed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, because it collects nothing.

Your choices

  • Export your data, or delete a profile, in the Me tab.
  • Turn off the camera in your phone's settings.
  • Clear the app's website data, or delete the app, to remove everything.
